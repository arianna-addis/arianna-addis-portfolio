About

Researcher,
trail runner.

I'm an organizational psychologist and applied researcher with 6+ years of experience studying how humans work — together, alongside machines, and through hardship. My research sits at the intersection of team dynamics, technology, and well-being, approached through a Humanitarian Work Psychology lens.

I earned my M.S. in I/O Psychology from Florida Institute of Technology (2024), where I studied trust in human-AI teams for the Air Force, team cohesion for the Army, and led qualitative research on the well-being of digital humanitarian volunteers. My B.Sc. in Psychology is from the University of Washington, where I also worked in research labs studying leadership, resilience, creativity, and social anxiety.

Between degrees, I hiked ~600 miles solo on the Pacific Crest Trail — navigating injuries, wildfires, and weather. It deepened everything I believe about resilience, grit, and what people are capable of. You'll find those themes throughout my research.

Outside of work I'm running, rock climbing, guiding backpacking trips at Two Sentinels Girl Scout Camp, or playing saxophone with the UW Husky Alumni Marching Band.

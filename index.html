Industry
Measuring marathoner grit from wearable data
Brooks Running · Seattle, WA · 2024

Runners don't just perform — they push, quit, dig deep, and come back. But how do you measure grit without interrupting a run? As a graduate intern at Brooks Running, I designed and led an original end-to-end research project to answer exactly that question. Starting with a 40+ article literature review on running psychology — covering flow state, running identity, embodiment, and wearable technology — I identified a gap: psychological states were poorly captured by existing tools. I proposed and built a new approach using runners' watch data (131,000 workouts) and survey responses (1,200 participants) via Python, SQL, and DashLX to unobtrusively measure psychological constructs including grit. The goal was practical: better identify ideal wear testers and inform product development strategy. I presented findings at the company-wide Research Symposium and to the Footwear Department. I also independently led a full end-to-end wear test cycle for a Tier 2 trail shoe — recruiting testers, managing shipping logistics, distributing surveys, analyzing 1st-impression and 2-week results, and presenting to key stakeholders.

131K workouts · 1,200 surveys Python · SQL · DashLX Research Symposium presentation

Federal research
Trust in human-AI teams
Air Force Office of Scientific Research · Florida Institute of Technology · 2021–2024

As AI systems become teammates rather than tools, trust becomes critical — and hard to measure. This $650K, 3-year federally funded project studied how trust forms, erodes, and repairs in human-agent teams (HATs) operating in complex, simulated environments. I joined as a Project Assistant, coding 35+ articles for a comprehensive literature review and translating 200 sources into two multi-level theoretical models of trust dynamics. I managed development and validation of 10 unobtrusive, behavioral trust measures using multimodal data streams — including heart rate (ECG), galvanic skin response (GSR), behavioral observation, and surveys — through a four-month, 200+ participant data collection effort. I ran 31 three-hour experimental sessions, merged and analyzed survey data across 7 timepoints and 9 conditions in R using slope modeling. Promoted to Project Lead in August 2023, I co-led the final year of the project — running weekly team and committee meetings, onboarding 3 new research assistants, and leading writing and revision of 4 scientific publications.

400+ participants 4 publications $650K grant · R · ECG · GSR

Federal research
Behavioral measures of team cohesion & resilience
Army Research Institute · Florida Institute of Technology · 2021–2024

What does team cohesion actually look like in the field — and can you measure it without asking teams to stop and fill out surveys? This multi-institution project, a cooperative agreement with the Army Research Institute, set out to build a high-fidelity answer. Collaborating with four universities and two applied research firms, the team developed a novel teams research paradigm to generate data for a real-world team composition algorithm for the U.S. Army. My role centered on behavioral data collection: I observed and recorded behavior for 5-person teams across 30 outdoor, 3-hour experimental sessions — 150+ participants in total. I contributed to the development and validation of unobtrusive measures of both team cohesion and team resilience based on multimodal behavioral data.

150+ participants · 30 sessions 6 collaborating institutions 90+ hrs of field sessions

Qualitative
Well-being of digital humanitarian aid workers
M.S. Thesis · Florida Institute of Technology · Advisor: Dr. Richard Griffith · 2024

When a crisis strikes, humanitarian workers mobilize — often digitally, often voluntarily, and often at a significant personal cost. My M.S. thesis asked: what does that work do to the people who do it? Using a qualitative, phenomenological methodology, I conducted in-depth interviews with digital humanitarian volunteers to explore how remote aid work shapes psychological well-being, resilience, and burnout over time. Thematic analysis surfaced common challenges volunteers face — boundary erosion, vicarious trauma, unclear role structure — alongside protective factors like community, purpose, and organizational support. This project required me to design my own study from scratch: developing interview protocols, securing IRB approval, recruiting participants, conducting interviews, and analyzing data using phenomenological methods.

Published M.S. thesis · May 2024 Phenomenological · IRB approved

Applied research
Girl Scout Voices Count national KPI survey
Girl Scout Research Institute · GSUSA · Summer 2022

How do you listen to 2.5 million members at once? The Girl Scout Research Institute's answer is the Girl Scout Voices Count survey — an annual national survey used to track KPIs including satisfaction, recommendation, and intent to stay across three customer groups: Girl Scouts, parents/caregivers, and Service Unit Volunteers. As a Research Fellow and Intern, I developed three 30-page research reports from 30,000+ survey responses, each tailored to a different audience. I used QDA Miner software to code 9,000 qualitative data points on troop leader pain points, analyzed quantitative DEI data across troop leaders, and examined cross-organizational partnership survey data for national leadership.

30K+ responses · 9K coded points 3 stakeholder reports · QDA Miner

International Association of Applied Psychology · 2022–2023

Mixed methods
LGBTQ+ experiences in work teams
Florida Institute of Technology · ICCM · 2021–2022

This mixed-methods project asked: what is it actually like to be LGBTQ+ in a work team, and how does identity shape the team experience at the meso level? I contributed to the full research lifecycle: developing semi-structured interview questions, creating a Qualtrics survey for quantitative data collection, conducting pilot interviews, and assisting with the broader interview process. The qualitative strand used thematic analysis to build a grounded theoretical framework of LGBTQ+ experiences in teams, while the quantitative strand used social network analysis to model team-level dynamics. Findings were accepted for presentation at SIOP Annual Conference in April 2023 in Boston.

Social network analysis · Qualtrics SIOP 2023 · Boston

About

Researcher,
trail runner.

I'm an organizational psychologist and applied researcher with 6+ years of experience studying how humans work — together, alongside machines, and through hardship. My work sits at the intersection of team dynamics, technology, and well-being, approached through a Humanitarian Work Psychology lens: not just asking how organizations can be more productive, but how work can be more humane.

I earned my M.S. in I/O Psychology from Florida Institute of Technology (2024), where I studied trust in human-AI teams for the Air Force, team cohesion for the Army Research Institute, and led independent qualitative research on the psychological well-being of digital humanitarian volunteers — under the supervision of Dr. Richard Griffith. My B.Sc. in Psychology is from the University of Washington, where I also worked across labs studying leadership development, resilience, creativity, and social anxiety.

Between degrees, I hiked 600 miles solo on the Pacific Crest Trail — navigating injuries, wildfires, and unpredictable terrain. It taught me more about grit, resilience, and human limits than any dataset could. You'll find those themes throughout my research.

Outside of work I'm running, rock climbing, guiding backpacking trips at Two Sentinels Girl Scout Camp, or playing tenor saxophone with the UW Husky Alumni Marching Band.
